Holy moly, term three was quite hectic. I have a lot of topics I want to write about but as a way to
destress from the last three months, I just wanted to perform some reflections on how term three
went for me so far in CST.

---


## Grateful

One of the things that I feel when thinking about term three is how grateful I am for having decided
to go back to school. There are some things that I still look back on and feel I should have done
different but I am quite confident being in school right now and doing the things I am doing can do
me no wrong. Each term in CST honestly feels like a mini developer bootcamp. Going back to my
time in BrainStation, it was a memorable three months. It was also a relatively difficult time just
due to the fact everyday felt like I was coding 8 am to 10 pm almost everyday. This sort of feels
like CST though albeit I can squeeze in proper breaks and rest days here and there. One of the
things that stood out to me regarding my term three was that I am now getting use to the motions and
rhythm of the program. 

## Grades Don't Matter

Of course, since I come from a dance background I think about things in rhythm and feeling lol.

One of things I have started to tell myself this term was that grades don't matter. While I
partially don't think that's always a good thing to say, one of the things I was starting to not be
able to manage well was the grind mindset I had going into term 1 and term 2. Aiming for almost
perfect grades in every quiz, assignment, and exam is very exhausting. I have learnt that if I
am willing to sacrifice my own personal time, time with friends, and with family/loved ones, then I
can definitely get the grades and projects that I want. However, I think the tradeoffs in the long
run despite CST being considered a "short-term" period of my life is not worth it. I realized this
term I needed to find balance or else I was probably going to be on a one-way stop to burnout.

## Preventing Burnout

One of the things I did a lot more this term was adjust my expectations and measure how much
effort I wanted to put. Even though grades don't matter this term (unless you are failing of course)
I still wanted to "learn".
